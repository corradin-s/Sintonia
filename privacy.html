Privacy

Last updated 20 November 2026

Sintonia is a small, independent app for listening to independent radio. It has no accounts, no advertising and no tracking cookies. It only counts, anonymously, how many people open it. This notice describes what the app’s code actually does with your data.

Who is responsible

Sofia Corradin is responsible for the data described here (the controller). You can write to her at sintoniaonair@gmail.com.

Opening the app

Sintonia is hosted on GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub, Inc. When you open it, GitHub receives your IP address, as any web server does, so that it can deliver the page. GitHub logs visitors’ IP addresses for security purposes.

The fonts and code Sintonia uses come from the same site. Nothing is loaded from Google Fonts or from other content delivery networks.

Sintonia doesn’t set cookies.

Counting how many people open it

Once a day, the first time you open Sintonia, it adds one line to a count kept in Sintonia’s Supabase project (hosted in the EU, Ireland). The line holds only:

  • the date and time;
  • whether it’s the first time Sintonia is opened on this phone;
  • whether it’s open from the Home Screen or in the browser;
  • the kind of link you came from (for example instagram, or direct), never the full address;
  • your phone’s time zone (for example Europe/Rome).

It holds no IP address, no random code and nothing that tells one person from another. A note on this phone (“already counted today”) keeps the same phone from being counted twice in a day. The count is only ever read as totals: per day, per kind of link, per time zone. Lines older than 13 months are deleted automatically.

In the same way, Sintonia counts how people answer when it asks whether they’d like to be on the map. Each answer adds one line that holds only the date and time, the answer (for example “chose a city” or “not now”), when the question came up (after a minute of listening, the first time the map opens, or from the map’s own button) and whether Sintonia is open from the Home Screen. It holds nothing that tells one person from another, is only read as totals, and is deleted after 13 months.

What’s on air, and listening

While Sintonia is open, it asks the stations and their broadcasting providers what’s on air and, for some, their published schedule, through their public APIs (NTS, radio.co, Airtime, Radio Cult, Live365 and AzuraCast servers), so that the cards can show it. When you play a station, your phone connects directly to that station’s streaming server.

Each of these servers receives your IP address, as any website does, and handles it under its own privacy policy. Sintonia doesn’t load photos or cover pictures from them, or from anywhere else: stations are shown only with their colours.

For some stations, the station’s page in Sintonia also lists past shows the station has published on Mixcloud. When you open that page, Sintonia asks Mixcloud’s public API for the latest shows, so Mixcloud receives your IP address. A past show plays through Mixcloud’s own player, which Sintonia loads when a station’s list of past shows is on screen, so that a show can start as soon as you tap it. Mixcloud handles your data, and any cookies of its own, under its privacy policy. Sintonia doesn’t show Mixcloud’s pictures either.

A station’s website, support page or shop, opened from its Info page, is outside Sintonia and follows its own policy. Sintonia earns nothing from these links.

The map and other listeners

When Sintonia opens, it connects to Supabase, the service that shows on the map how many other people are listening and roughly where, and passes on reactions. Supabase receives your IP address. Supabase keeps a technical log of each connection, including your IP address, for one day. Sintonia’s Supabase project is hosted in the EU (Ireland).

Your own dot is shared only after you choose your city when Sintonia asks “Where are you listening from?” (or from the map), or, if you prefer, after you let your phone share its location. Choosing a city needs no GPS and no permission from the phone. From then on, whenever Sintonia is open, it sends:

  • your position, rounded to half a degree (about 50 km);
  • the station you’re playing, if any;
  • a random code, so that reopening the app doesn’t count you twice.

Other people with Sintonia open see this as an anonymous dot. It isn’t written to any database: it exists only in Supabase’s live channel and disappears when you close the app.

So that the map also shows where people have been listening, Sintonia adds, at most once every three hours, one line to Sintonia’s Supabase project with only your position rounded to half a degree (about 50 km) and the time. The line holds no random code and nothing that tells one person from another or links one line to the next. For 24 hours, other people see that place as a faint ring, without a time or a number. The lines are deleted automatically after two days.

While a station is playing, you can send a reaction: Love, Fire, Groove or Vibe. It goes through the same live channel with the station you’re playing and, only if you share your dot, the same rounded position. People with Sintonia open at that moment see it cross the map, without knowing who sent it. The station itself doesn’t receive it. Reactions aren’t written to any database: they disappear after a few seconds.

On your phone, Sintonia keeps the city you chose, or, if you used the phone’s location, your position rounded to about 10 km, so that the map can show where you are. Only in that second case, while location access is allowed, it refreshes the position when you open the app.

To stop, tap “Stop sharing my location” on the map: your dot disappears for everyone and the saved position is deleted from this phone. A faint ring already sent fades within 24 hours and is deleted after two days. You can join the map again whenever you like.

What stays on your phone

Sintonia keeps the following in your browser’s storage on this phone. None of it is sent anywhere, except what the counting and map sections describe:

  • your first name, if you give it, and whether Sintonia has already asked for it;
  • your theme and background, and the colours you chose for story pictures;
  • the last station you played;
  • your pinned stations;
  • the songs you’ve heard, under Recently heard;
  • how long you’ve listened to each station, to sort Most listened;
  • your city or rounded position, if you joined the map, until you tap “Stop sharing my location”, and when the last faint ring was sent;
  • whether Sintonia has already asked if you’d like to be on the map;
  • the random code used on the map;
  • the stations’ published schedules, to show when they’re next live, kept for a day;
  • your settings;
  • the day Sintonia was last counted, and whether this phone has been counted yet;
  • a short-lived note used when the app updates itself, cleared when you close it.

To delete them one by one, clear Recently heard from its sheet, unpin stations, empty your name in Settings, or tap “Stop sharing my location” on the map. To delete everything at once, clear Sintonia’s website data in your browser’s settings, and if you added Sintonia to your Home Screen, delete it from there too.

Story pictures

The picture for Instagram stories is drawn on your phone. Sintonia doesn’t upload it anywhere: it goes only where you choose to share or save it.

Legal bases

Sharing your dot on the map, live and as a faint ring, is based on your consent (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR), which you give by choosing your city or by letting your phone share its location. You can withdraw it at any time with “Stop sharing my location” on the map. Withdrawing doesn’t affect what was shared before.

Everything else (delivering the app, showing what’s on air, playing stations and their past shows, counting how many people open it and how they answer the map question, showing how many people are listening, passing on the reactions you send) is based on the legitimate interest in running a working radio app (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Providers outside the EU

GitHub and Supabase are providers based outside the European Union (GitHub, Inc. in the United States), so your IP address may be processed in the United States. They apply the safeguards set out in their data processing agreements. Their own privacy policies: GitHub, Supabase. Mixcloud, used only for past shows, is Mixcloud Ltd, based in the United Kingdom; its privacy policy is linked above.

Sintonia itself has no server and keeps no records about you: the count of opens holds nothing that identifies you.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask to access, correct or delete your data, to restrict or object to its processing, and to receive it in a portable form. You can also withdraw your consent at any time. Sintonia keeps nothing about you on a server, so most of your data is on your phone, where you can delete it yourself. For anything else, write to sintoniaonair@gmail.com.

You can also lodge a complaint with the Italian data protection authority, the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali, or with the authority in the country where you live or work.

For radio stations

Sintonia is an independent project and isn’t affiliated with any of the stations it lists. It plays each station’s public stream from the station’s own servers and shows the information the station publishes. It carries no advertising and doesn’t record broadcasts.

If you run a station and would like something changed or removed, write to sintoniaonair@gmail.com.
